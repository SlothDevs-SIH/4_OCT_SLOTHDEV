# Datasets: what Catalyst AI uses, why, and what each one is for

Catalyst AI is for **home-business owners of any kind** (the first case study is Box Box, an F1 merchandise seller) who want analytics to focus on next month's sales. No public dataset contains a home business's own private numbers, so we combine three kinds of data, each clearly labelled:

| Kind | What | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Public datasets** | Real, published data used to *validate* the engine and to *calibrate* how generated data behaves | `public`, with source and licence |
| **Generated demo businesses** | Box Box and a second sample (a home baker), generated by us from the public data's shapes plus explicit scenario assumptions | `synthetic: true`, always |
| **The user's own data** | What a real founder uploads or pastes | `real` |

**Rule:** public data is never presented as a user's data, and generated data is never presented as real. Every number shown to a user carries its source.

## 2. Each dataset in detail

### 2.1 Olist Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset (the closest fit for small sellers)
- **What it is:** about 100,000 orders placed on the Olist marketplace across Brazil, 4 Sep 2016 to 17 Oct 2018, with items, prices, freight, payments, delivery dates and customer reviews. Nine CSV files.
- **Source and licence:** Kaggle, `olistbr/brazilian-ecommerce`. **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, so non-commercial. A Kaggle login is needed to download. Cite: Olist (2018).
- **Measured on the real file** (`backend/data_engine/external/snapshots/olist_profile.json`):

| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Orders / sellers / categories | 99,441 / 3,095 / 73 |
| Orders per seller | median **6**; 1,824 sellers under 10 orders; 842 with 10 to 49; **403 with 50 to 500**; 26 above 500 |
| Share of seller orders from the 50 to 500 band | 50.9% |
| Repeat customers (2+ orders) | **3.1%** of all customers; **2.1%** for customers of the 50 to 500 sellers |
| Late deliveries | 8.1% of delivered orders; median delivery 10.2 days, 90th percentile 23.1 |
| Review score | mean 4.09; 14.7% scored 1 or 2; 41% include a comment |
| Item price (BRL) | median 75, 90th percentile 230; freight is 16.6% of item price |
| Top categories (order lines) | bed/bath/table, health/beauty, sports/leisure, furniture/decor, computers/accessories |

- **What it gives us:** the realistic **low repeat rate** of small sellers (this is why "repeat orders" is a real bottleneck), a realistic spread of seller sizes (the brief's "50 to 500 orders" band exists and is large), delivery delay and review behaviour for the **capacity** and trust signals, and proof that the engine is not shirt-specific: it spans 73 categories.
- **Limits:** a marketplace in Brazil (BRL), not Instagram or WhatsApp. It has **no friend or stranger tag, no unit costs and no Instagram insights**. Non-commercial licence. Never presented as a user's data.

### 2.2 UCI Online Retail II
- **What it is:** every transaction of a UK-based online gift-ware retailer, 1 Dec 2009 to 9 Dec 2011. Many customers are wholesalers.
- **Source and licence:** UCI Machine Learning Repository, **CC BY 4.0**. Cite: Chen, D. (2012), doi:10.24432/C5CG6D. Direct download, no login.
- **Measured** (`retail_ii_profile.json`): 1,067,371 lines (exactly UCI's published count); 22% of lines have no customer id; 1.8% are cancellations; 39,516 valid orders from 5,852 customers; repeat customers 72.4%; median 25 days between a customer's orders (90th percentile 136); top 10 customers are 16.0% of revenue; 23.1% of customers make 80% of revenue.
- **What it gives us:** a **messy, real order file** to test intake. Running it found two real importer bugs (an "Invoice" or "Bill No" column was not recognised as the order id, and a sheet without a campaign column was wrongly penalised). The same order metrics (repeat share, days between orders, concentration) run on 1M lines in about 16 seconds and are reused on a founder's 50 to 500 orders. Our import pipeline loads all 39,516 invoices with 0 quarantined.
- **Limits:** a wholesaler (72% repeat), in pounds, so it is **not a benchmark** for home businesses. Olist is the better reference for that.

### 2.3 UCI Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention
- **What it is:** 12,330 website sessions of an online shop, one per user over a year, each labelled with whether it ended in a purchase, with visitor type, pages visited, bounce rate and page value.
- **Source:** UCI, Sakar and Kastro (2018), doi:10.24432/C5F88Q. UCI's metadata lists no licence: confirm the terms on the UCI page. Cited, not redistributed.
- **Measured** (`shoppers_profile.json`): 15.5% of sessions end in a purchase. **New visitors convert at 24.9%, returning visitors at 13.9%** (1.8 times). Sessions that saw pages with value convert at **56.3% vs 3.9%** (14.6 times). More product pages viewed means higher conversion (6.2%, 17.7%, 25.3%).
- **What it gives us:** the closest public analogue to "new people vs people the shop already knows", and the conversion step. It supports the lead score's design direction: **intent outweighs light interest** and **new people behave differently**.
- **Limits:** one shop, sessions not leads, and its new-visitor effect is specific to this shop.

### 2.4 UCI Bank Marketing (the lead-score challenger)
- **What it is:** phone campaigns of a Portuguese bank (41,188 rows, May 2008 to Nov 2010), labelled with whether the client subscribed.
- **Source:** UCI, Moro, Cortez and Rita (2014). See the UCI page for terms. Cited, not redistributed.
- **How it is used:** the lead score starts as transparent hand-set points (see `BACKEND_1_DATA_ENGINE.md`). We trained a logistic regression on **contact history only** (previous outcome, prior contacts, days since last contact, contacts this campaign) as a learned **challenger and sanity check**. On a strictly later hold-out of 2,700 rows it scores PR-AUC **0.680 vs 0.512** for a no-skill baseline (ROC-AUC 0.68). Its learned direction agrees with the points: a previous success scores highest, and a longer gap lowers the score.
- **Honest limits:** a different industry and only 4 inputs. We rank leads with it, we do not claim it models home businesses. The bank's conversion rate also drifted over time (17.8% to 51.2% across periods), so its probabilities are not calibrated to any home business. Details are in the model card and `lead_model_report.json`.

### 2.5 F1 race calendar and Wikipedia page views (market context)
- **Calendar:** Jolpica-F1 (the open successor of the Ergast API), no key. The 2026 snapshot has 23 races (6 sprint weekends); 2025 has 24. Each race has its weekend start and end.
- **Page views:** daily views of the English Wikipedia article "Formula One", 1 Jan 2025 to 3 Oct 2026, CC0.
- **Measured:** mean daily views on race-weekend days are **2.0 times** other days in both seasons (2025: 13,197 vs 6,583; 2026: 9,408 vs 4,738; 119 weekend days vs 522 other days).
- **What it gives us:** for the Box Box case, race weekends are demand windows, so the advisor can time a drop, post or collaboration and cite a number. It shows how a **market context module** works: any calendar can plug in the same way.
- **Limits:** page views are interest in F1 in general, not any brand's sales. Used for Box Box only.

## 3. What is covered, and what is a scenario assumption

| Bottleneck | Public data that informs it | What is NOT in any public data (a scenario assumption or the user's own data) |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | Olist (seller sizes); Online Shoppers (new vs returning) | Friend, friend of a friend, stranger: **no public data has this**. Comes from the user's tag, or a stated assumption |
| **Conversion** | Online Shoppers (conversion, intent vs interest) | A home business's own Instagram reach and profile visits |
| **Margin** | Olist (price and freight levels) | The founder's unit costs |
| **Repeat orders** | Olist (2% to 3% small-seller repeat), Online Retail II (gaps between orders) | The business's own repeat behaviour |
| **Capacity** | Olist (delivery delays and late share) | The founder's own limits |
| **Lead quality** | Bank Marketing (contact history), Online Shoppers (intent signals) | Real DM outcomes (learned weekly from the user's own data) |
| **Timing** | F1 calendar and page views (Box Box); festival calendar (planned) | Any individual's sales |

## 4. The generated demo businesses

**Why generate:** no public dataset has a home business's friend or stranger split, unit costs or Instagram insights, and a real founder's data may not be available today.

**Box Box** (F1 merchandise) and **a home baker** (to show the product is not shirt-specific) are generated by the same engine, about 240 orders each over about 14 weeks, deterministic (same seed, same data), always labelled `synthetic: true`.

**What is calibrated on public data (real shapes):**
| Behaviour | Calibrated on |
|---|---|
| Order value spread and basket size | Olist, Online Retail II |
| Gaps between a customer's orders | Online Retail II (median 25 days, scaled down for a small business) |
| Repeat share | Olist's 2% to 3% as the floor, and a stated scenario value above it |
| Delivery delays and late share | Olist (median 10 days, 8% late) |
| New vs returning conversion | Online Shoppers (24.9% vs 13.9%) |
| How intent and history raise the chance of buying | Online Shoppers and Bank Marketing (directions) |
| Demand on race weekends (Box Box) | F1 calendar and the measured 2.0 times interest uplift, labelled "interest, not sales" |

**What is an explicit scenario assumption (labelled in the data card, and changeable):**
the share of orders from friends vs strangers (Box Box: most orders from friends and friends of friends, a few from strangers, because that is the case the brief describes), unit costs, Instagram reach per post, and the capacity limit. These are stated so nobody mistakes them for measured facts.

**Rules:** deterministic generator, `synthetic: true` on every row and response, the scenario assumptions listed in the data card, and public data never shown as the user's.

## 6. Access and licence compliance

Raw files live in `data/raw/` and are **git-ignored; we never redistribute them**. We cite each source. Committed to the repo are only small derived **profiles** (aggregate numbers) and the F1 and page-view snapshots (factual schedule data and CC0 counts).

```bash
python -m backend.data_engine.ml.fetch_datasets            # Bank Marketing
python -m backend.data_engine.ml.fetch_datasets --retail   # Online Retail II (45 MB)
python -m backend.data_engine.external.shoppers --fetch --profile
# Olist: download from Kaggle (login), unzip the 9 CSVs into data/raw/olist/ (or data/raw/), then
python -m backend.data_engine.external.olist --profile
python -m backend.data_engine.external.retail --convert --profile
python -m backend.data_engine.external.f1_calendar --refresh 2025 2026
python -m backend.data_engine.external.f1_interest --refresh
```

Olist is non-commercial. Catalyst AI is free for everyone, which stays within the licence for a hackathon prototype, but anything commercial later must not depend on it.
